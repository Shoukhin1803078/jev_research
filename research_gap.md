# Research Gap Analysis — Reducing Latency in RAG

Source papers: 22 downloaded PDFs (`papers/`) across 5 categories, analyzed folder-by-folder. One paper (A2C-RAG) had no open-access version and is excluded. Target experimental setup: open-source small LLMs (Qwen 8B, small Llama/HF models), single consumer/workstation GPU, no large-scale pretraining.

---

## Overall synthesis (read this first)

**The field splits RAG-latency work into four largely separate axes, and almost nobody combines two of them in one system:**

1. **KV-cache reuse** (01) — reuse/recompute cached key-value states to cut prefill/TTFT.
2. **Prefetching/pipelining** (02) — overlap retrieval with generation, predict what to fetch ahead of time.
3. **Context compression** (03) — shrink what's fed to the decoder (tokens or embeddings).
4. **Visual-document retrieval compression** (04) — a parallel, smaller literature for multimodal/ColPali-style retrieval.

Both surveys (05) confirm this from the outside: the RAG survey explicitly treats efficiency as "one axis among five" rather than a unified research question, and the Agentic RAG survey shows latency scales with architectural complexity but offers no numbers for small-model/single-GPU regimes. **Across all 22 papers, essentially every strong empirical result is reported on 7B–70B+ models on A100/H100 clusters or data-center-scale multi-accelerator setups.** Very few papers report single-query, single-consumer-GPU numbers at all (TeleRAG and VoiceAgentRAG are the clear exceptions). This is itself the biggest structural gap: **nobody has systematically measured how these latency techniques' cost/benefit trade-offs change once you move to the small-model, single-GPU, real-local-generation regime** — most techniques were tuned and validated where GPU memory and compute headroom are abundant, which is the opposite of your setup.

### The clearest, most repeated specific gap across folders
**No paper stacks a *learned/predictive* layer with a *systems-level/infrastructure* layer.** This exact pattern repeats three times independently:
- In 01 (KV-cache): semantic token-selection methods (SpecCache's speculative-model proxy, CacheBlend's deviation scoring, CacheTune's frequency-domain selection, FusionRAG's similarity-guided selection) are never combined with multi-tier storage/prefetch infrastructure (PCR, CacheTune's hardware-aware tuner).
- In 02 (prefetching): learned retrieval-need predictors (Predictive Prefetching, VoiceAgentRAG's Slow Thinker) are never combined with GPU-memory-aware prefetch scheduling (TeleRAG's lookahead retrieval, HedraRAG's partial GPU caching).
- In 03 (compression): architecture-level compression that changes what the decoder attends to (REFRAG's embeddings) is never combined with task-performance-driven selection (CORE-RAG's RL-on-accuracy) or evidence filtering (ECoRAG/ACC-RAG).

### A second repeated gap: nobody tests the "generation-dominated" local regime
VoiceAgentRAG explicitly admits that with an API-based LLM, generation latency (500–8000ms) completely swamps their 110ms retrieval savings, and says faster/local inference is needed to make retrieval savings visible. Your planned setup — fast, local Qwen8B/Llama generation — is **exactly the condition under which this becomes testable**, and no paper in this set has done it.

### A third gap: adaptive/query-conditioned resource allocation is consistently flagged, never solved
ACC-RAG's selector is explicitly named by its own authors as "the largest bottleneck." DocPC's page budget is fixed and shown to be non-monotonic (more pages can hurt). HPC-ColPali's pruning ratio is a fixed manual hyperparameter. PULSAR's pooling is static/uniform, not content-adaptive. This is a cross-category pattern: **every "compress/select/prefetch how much" decision is currently either fixed or a weak, separately-trained heuristic — none are jointly optimized end-to-end against downstream latency+accuracy.**

### Candidate fusion directions (ranked by single-GPU feasibility)

1. **Compress-then-cache** (03 + 01 fusion, high feasibility): Use a cheap adaptive compressor (ECoRAG-style evidentiality filtering, ~110M+770M params, or ACC-RAG's LoRA-based compressor — both run comfortably alongside Qwen8B on one GPU) to shrink retrieved context, then store/reuse the *compressed* chunk's KV cache (CacheBlend/CacheTune-style selective recompute) instead of compressing online every time. No paper in 01 or 03 combines these two layers. This directly targets the "orthogonal axes, never combined" gap stated explicitly in both folders.
2. **Lookahead prefetch + selective KV recompute** (02 + 01 fusion, high feasibility): Combine TeleRAG's lookahead retrieval (validated on a single RTX4090 with Llama-3-8B, open code) with CacheBlend or SpecCache's selective recompute once the prefetched chunk's KV cache needs cross-chunk correction. Both are training-free / lightweight and built on compatible serving stacks (vLLM/FAISS).
3. **Local generation-dominance study** (02 fusion, very high feasibility, lower technical novelty but genuinely unfilled): Reproduce VoiceAgentRAG's dual-agent prefetch-cache architecture (framework-agnostic, supports Ollama/local models per their own code) fully locally with Qwen8B, and measure whether retrieval-latency savings become the dominant, visible effect once generation is local and fast — directly testing a limitation the original authors flagged themselves.
4. **Visual-document stacked compression** (04 fusion, moderate feasibility, less crowded niche): Combine DocPC's document-level representative-page selection with HPC-ColPali's attention-guided patch pruning/quantization — no paper in 04 stacks the page-count axis and the patch-count axis, and HPC-ColPali's own numbers are admittedly simulated, so even a straight single-axis replication is a legitimate contribution.

**Recommendation:** direction 1 (compress-then-cache) or 2 (prefetch + selective recompute) are the strongest fits — both have multiple open-source, single-GPU-validated building blocks (ECoRAG/ACC-RAG for compression; CacheBlend/TeleRAG for caching/prefetch) to build on without needing REFRAG- or TurboRAG-scale fine-tuning budgets, and both target a gap every relevant paper explicitly leaves open rather than one inferred post hoc.

## 1. Prefill / KV-Cache Reuse

### FusionRAG / Fusion RAG Cache (SIGMOD 2026)
- **Core idea:** Offline "similarity-guided" preprocessing embeds cross-chunk context into each chunk's KV cache, and online "query-guided" selection picks critical tokens for cheap recomputation via a custom sparse-attention kernel (Q-Sparse-Attn), closing the quality gap left by prior selective-recompute methods.
- **Key reported result:** At <15% recomputation, up to 70% higher normalized-F1 than CacheBlend/Cache-Craft; TTFT reduced 2.66–9.39x vs Full Attention; Q-Sparse-Attn gives ~4.2x speedup over FlashAttention at 15% recompute; throughput 1.2–4.3x.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Offline preprocessing adds 0.218s/chunk, which becomes non-negligible cumulative overhead for continuously/frequently updated, high-volume corpora.
  - Explicitly admits it underperforms in "write-once, read-once" scenarios and is best suited to stable, frequently-queried corpora (e.g., enterprise KBs).
- **Research gap (my analysis):**
  - No mechanism for incremental/streaming corpus updates — offline preprocessing cost is amortized only under repeated querying, unsolved for live-news-style ingestion.
  - Custom kernel (Q-Sparse-Attn) ties the speedup to a specific attention implementation; portability/maintenance cost of such kernels across model families is unaddressed.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Training-free, evaluated directly on Qwen2.5-7B/14B and Qwen3-32B on single/few A100-class GPUs — very reproducible for a Qwen8B single-GPU setup; main barrier is implementing/porting the custom sparse-attention kernel.

### SpecCache (ACL 2026)
- **Core idea:** Uses a lightweight speculative model's deep-layer hidden-state norms as a cheap proxy to identify which tokens in the target model's KV cache need recomputation, since deep-layer features align strongly across model families.
- **Key reported result:** TTFT reduced 2.17–3.95x vs full recompute, throughput up 2.7–5.2x; outperforms CacheBlend by 2.1–23.2% and EPIC by 3.4–30.08% in task scores at equal recompute ratios.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Uses a generic, off-the-shelf speculative model rather than one fine-tuned to mimic the target's attention patterns.
  - Evaluation concentrated on 7B–13B models; scalability to 70B-parameter architectures and broader domain-specific datasets is untested.
  - Authors note quality is "not strictly monotonic" with recomputation ratio — more recompute can occasionally hurt (e.g., on MuSiQue), especially with cross-tokenizer alignment noise.
- **Research gap (my analysis):**
  - Running a second (speculative) model adds GPU memory/compute overhead that is never quantified against single-model alternatives (CacheTune, FusionRAG) under identical hardware.
  - No combination with multi-tier storage/prefetching (PCR, CacheTune) — assumes KV caches already resident in fast memory.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Needs two models co-resident on one GPU (e.g., Llama3-1B as speculator + Qwen8B target) — feasible on a single consumer GPU but adds memory pressure; built on vLLM, train-free, reproducible.

### TurboRAG (EMNLP 2025)
- **Core idea:** Precomputes chunk-level KV caches offline using independent (non-cross-chunk) attention and reordered RoPE positions, then lightly fine-tunes the LLM to adapt to this stitched-cache attention pattern at inference.
- **Key reported result:** TTFT reduced up to 9.4x (avg 8.6x) vs naive RAG with comparable accuracy after fine-tuning (within ~1% on RGB/LongBench); ~98.5% reduction in online compute (TFLOPs).
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Requires supervised fine-tuning per base model (~888 GPU-hours on 32 A100s, ~$3,000 for a 7B model) to avoid accuracy regression; without fine-tuning, quality drop reaches ~20% on harder benchmarks (composite positions) and still a few % with reordered positions.
  - Authors explicitly flag unresolved practical deployment issues: managing dynamic/updating knowledge bases, versioned cache entries, incremental offline pipelines, and graceful online-prefill fallback.
- **Research gap (my analysis):** Entirely ignores cross-chunk attention (zero recompute) rather than selectively restoring it like CacheBlend/SpecCache/CacheTune/FusionRAG — no quality recovery mechanism, so gains come purely from fine-tuning compensation, not architectural fidelity.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** The SFT step is the real barrier — authors used 32 A100 GPUs; reproducing full fine-tuning on a single consumer GPU for Qwen8B would require LoRA/PEFT substitution and reduced batch size, with uncertain fidelity to reported results.

### PCR / Prefetch-Enhanced Cache Reuse (2026)
- **Core idea:** A pure-systems KV-cache reuse stack — prefix-tree caching with look-ahead LRU, layer-wise pipelined CPU/SSD↔GPU transfer, and queue-based SSD→DRAM prefetching — to maximize exact-prefix cache reuse across a GPU/DRAM/SSD hierarchy without touching model quality.
- **Key reported result:** Up to 2.47x TTFT speedup vs SOTA KV-reuse baselines (SCCache/LMCache/vLLM); average TTFT reduction of 36.4% (Llama2-7B) to 50.9% (Llama2-13B) vs SCCache; P99 tail latency cut by >30%.
- **Stated limitations (implied/discussed, no explicit "Limitations" section):**
  - Relies on exact prefix matching, so cache hit rate is still capped by RAG's combinatorial document-ordering problem (n! chunk orders) — the paper itself frames this as the motivating weakness of prior prefix-caching work, which PCR does not fundamentally fix, only mitigates via storage capacity.
  - SSD read speed can make cache loading slower than recomputation for very large KV caches in some configurations.
  - Optimal prefetch window size is hardware/model-dependent and requires manual profiling (no universal default).
- **Research gap (my analysis):** Purely an infrastructure/I-O optimization — orthogonal to and never combined with semantic selective-recomputation methods (CacheBlend/SpecCache/CacheTune) that would raise the effective hit rate beyond exact-prefix matching.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Built on vLLM, tested on consumer-class hardware (RTX 4090 + NVMe SSD) with Llama/Qwen 7–14B models — directly reproducible on a single local GPU, no fine-tuning needed.

### CacheTune / Adaptive KV Cache Reuse (2026)
- **Core idea:** Applies FFT to the KV cache along the sequence dimension and shows global cross-chunk semantics concentrate in low-frequency components; selectively recomputes only the low-frequency-dominant KV pairs, with deferred RoPE recovery and a hardware-aware (roofline + golden-section search) recomputation-ratio tuner across GPU/CPU/SSD/HDD tiers.
- **Key reported result:** TTFT speedup 3.72–4.86x vs full recompute, throughput up 3.93–6.21x, ~94.8% quality retention on average; on slow SSD/HDD storage still sustains 2.34–2.36x TTFT speedup via adaptively raised recomputation ratio.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):** Explicit "Limitations" paragraph: "When the external storage bandwidth is extremely low or the asynchronous overlap window is insufficient, the system may still be limited by I/O." Authors also flag untested higher-concurrency, CXL-based disaggregated memory, and more complex workloads as future work.
- **Research gap (my analysis):**
  - Hardware-aware tuning needs a one-time per-deployment calibration (3–4 min profiling+search) — not zero-shot across hardware changes.
  - Low-frequency-energy selection is a heuristic with no theoretical optimality guarantee, and is never compared against SpecCache's speculative-model-based selection on identical setups.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Training-free, lightweight FFT profiling (~0.69ms/chunk); evaluated on Mistral-7B/Llama3-8B/Qwen2.5-32B across GPU/CPU/SSD/HDD tiers — highly reproducible on a single modest GPU with Qwen8B-class models.

### CoinRAG (2026)
- **Core idea:** Nugget-level (sub-chunk span) KV cache reuse: offline nugget extraction and nugget-aware fine-tuning, a two-stage chunk-to-nugget retrieval pipeline, and contextualized/position-aligned KV composition to minimize active context length under a strict TTFT SLA.
- **Key reported result:** Under a 100ms P99 latency budget, outperforms TurboRAG's F1 by 4.7–14.6% (avg 5.3%); without a latency limit still beats TurboRAG by 5.2% F1 with 6.8x shorter average context; context up to 10.1x shorter than Standard RAG.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself, explicit "Limitations" section):**
  - Offline costs scale with corpus size (disk storage, memory-mapped I/O needs) plus a non-trivial one-time nugget-aware fine-tuning cost.
  - The precomputed cache is tightly coupled to the specific model checkpoint — updating the backbone LLM forces full re-encoding of all documents.
  - Quality is bounded by chunk/nugget-level retrieval recall; also, nuggets from different chunks do not attend to each other during encoding (same chunk-isolation limitation as TurboRAG) — authors flag combining with CacheBlend-style cross-chunk attention as future work.
  - Evaluation assumes a capacity-limited cache and does not study inter-query KV-cache reuse across shared retrieved documents.
- **Research gap (my analysis):** No existing method combines CoinRAG's fine-grained nugget compression with CacheBlend/SpecCache-style cross-chunk attention recovery — explicitly left open by the authors.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Requires nugget-aware fine-tuning (tested on Qwen2.5-7B/14B, Qwen3-32B) — feasible on a single GPU via LoRA/PEFT at smaller scale, though full-scale reproduction of fine-tuning cost is nontrivial.

### CacheBlend (EuroSys 2025)
- **Core idea:** Selectively recomputes only the small fraction of tokens with the highest KV deviation per layer ("HKVD tokens," identified from first-layer deviation) to cheaply restore cross-chunk attention, pipelining recomputation with KV cache loading from slower storage tiers.
- **Key reported result:** Reduces TTFT by 2.2–3.3x vs full KV recompute, increases throughput by 2.8–5x, with only 0.01–0.03 F1/Rouge-L quality drop vs full recompute, across Mistral-7B/Yi-34B/Llama-70B.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself, explicit "Limitations and Future Work" section):**
  - Only applies to transformer architectures; investigation of non-transformer models (Mamba, Griffin) left for future work.
  - Not yet tested on newer serving engines (DistServe, StableGen) or across compute nodes sharing KV cache.
  - Hasn't tested different quantization settings or more models/datasets.
- **Research gap (my analysis):** Later papers (FusionRAG, SpecCache) show CacheBlend's first-layer-deviation token selection is biased toward early-position/early-chunk tokens and is suboptimal — no paper in this set head-to-head benchmarks all the differing selection criteria (first-layer deviation, deep-layer speculative proxy, frequency-domain) under identical conditions.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Training-free, open-sourced on top of vLLM/LMCache — the most directly reproducible baseline for a single-GPU Qwen8B/small-Llama experimental setup.

### RAGCache (2024)
- **Core idea:** A multilevel (GPU + host memory) dynamic caching system organizing retrieved-document KV caches in a prefix-aware knowledge tree with a PGDSF (Prefix-aware Greedy-Dual-Size-Frequency) eviction policy, plus cache-aware request reordering and dynamic speculative pipelining to overlap retrieval with generation.
- **Key reported result:** Reduces TTFT by up to 4x vs vLLM+Faiss and improves throughput up to 2.1x; vs SGLang, up to 3.5x lower TTFT and 1.8x higher throughput.
- **Stated limitations (implied/discussed, no explicit "Limitations" section):** The paper itself identifies that RAG systems are "sensitive to the referred order of retrieved documents" — KV cache for [D1,D2] differs from [D2,D1], so cache reuse fragments badly when document order varies across queries; this is presented as an open problem the knowledge-tree design only partially mitigates, not solves.
- **Research gap (my analysis):**
  - The order-sensitivity problem is explicitly named but left unsolved here — it directly motivates later non-prefix reuse work (TurboRAG, CacheBlend, FusionRAG) that this paper does not address.
  - Focuses solely on TTFT; decode-time latency (TPOT) is explicitly called out as a separate, unaddressed concern.
  - Speculative pipelining analysis assumes a simplified single-request-at-a-time model; the paper itself notes the general batched case is "more complex" and unresolved.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Implemented on vLLM+Faiss with Mistral-7B/LLaMA2-7B on a single A10G-class GPU — directly reproducible locally with small open-source models, no fine-tuning required.

## Cross-cutting gaps within KV-cache reuse (01_prefill_kv_cache_reuse)
- **Prefix-match vs. non-prefix reuse remains an unresolved trade-off**: RAGCache and PCR maximize exact-prefix cache hit rate and storage/I-O efficiency but never restore cross-chunk attention or escape the chunk-ordering combinatorics problem; CacheBlend/SpecCache/CacheTune/FusionRAG/CoinRAG/TurboRAG solve the ordering problem via recomputation or fine-tuning but largely assume fast/simple storage. No paper combines a multi-tier prefetching infrastructure (PCR/CacheTune-style) with the best semantic token-selection algorithm (SpecCache's speculative-model proxy or FusionRAG's similarity-guided preprocessing) into one system.
- **No head-to-head benchmark of selection criteria**: first-layer KV deviation (CacheBlend), deep-layer speculative-model hidden-state norms (SpecCache), frequency-domain low-pass energy (CacheTune), and similarity-guided offline + query-guided online selection (FusionRAG) are each proposed as superior to the others but are never compared under identical models/hardware/datasets — it's unclear which generalizes best for small 8B-class models under tight latency budgets.
- **Model-checkpoint coupling is unsolved**: Methods relying on fine-tuning or learned adaptation (TurboRAG, CoinRAG, KVLink as cited baseline) tie the precomputed cache to a specific model checkpoint; switching or updating/quantizing the model forces full corpus re-encoding, and no paper in this set offers a generic solution.
- **Corpus freshness vs. caching benefit is unaddressed**: nearly all methods assume a stable, repeatedly-queried corpus; FusionRAG explicitly admits weakness on "write-once, read-only" or high-churn corpora, and none of the eight papers provides a validated incremental/streaming cache-invalidation mechanism.
- **Storage-tier-aware adaptive recomputation is rare**: only CacheTune and PCR treat recomputation ratio / prefetch scheduling as jointly optimized with storage-tier speed (GPU/CPU/SSD/HDD); CacheBlend, TurboRAG, SpecCache, FusionRAG, and CoinRAG assume a relatively fast/simple hierarchy, leaving a gap for genuinely resource-constrained single-GPU/limited-RAM local setups.
- **TTFT optimization dominates; decode-time (TPOT) and concurrency are secondary or absent**: every paper's headline numbers are about prefill/TTFT; RAGCache explicitly notes TPOT as a distinct, unaddressed concern, and none study joint optimization of KV-cache reuse with continuous-batching/concurrent-request scheduling at the scale a production multi-tenant system would require.

## 2. Overlapping, Prefetching and Pipelining

### Predictive Prefetching for RAG (ICML 2026)
- **Core idea:** A lightweight auxiliary module (retrieval predictor + context monitor + query generator, ~62M added params) monitors transformer hidden states/attention/entropy to predict 8–16 tokens ahead of time *when* retrieval will be needed and *what* query to issue, enabling fully asynchronous, non-blocking prefetching instead of reactive/fixed-interval triggering.
- **Key reported result:** 43.5% end-to-end latency reduction, 62.4% TTFT reduction (287ms→108ms) on Llama-3.1-8B across HotpotQA/2WikiHop/NQ/TriviaQA, with answer quality within 0.5–2% of synchronous RAG and 31% fewer retrievals (AUROC 0.81 for predicting retrieval need).
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Requires access to LLM hidden states/attention/value vectors — cannot work with closed-API models (GPT-4, Claude); logit-only fallback drops to 0.66 AUROC.
  - Needs 50–100K generation traces for training the predictor; domain-specific calibration of thresholds (θ, Δ, τ_rag) is manual.
  - Assumes relatively predictable retrieval latency (100–500ms); highly variable network/API conditions degrade benefits.
  - 38.7% of triggered prefetches are false positives (28% fully wasted, adding ~8% latency overhead already baked into reported numbers).
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No handling of generation-dominated or very-short-output workloads where there's not enough lead time (8–16 tokens) to hide retrieval latency.
  - Reduction of wasted/false-positive prefetches is explicitly left as "future work" — no mechanism for canceling/reusing speculative fetches across branches.
  - Combination with system-level batching/caching (TeleRAG/HedraRAG) not explored — this is purely an algorithmic/decoding-time technique, orthogonal to serving infra.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Highly feasible — requires only white-box access to hidden states/attention (available for any local HF model like Qwen8B/Llama), a small transformer encoder, and a fine-tuned T5-small query generator; no large-scale pretraining needed. Main cost is collecting 50–100K oracle-labeled generation traces, which is reproducible at small scale with a single GPU, just time-consuming.

### TeleRAG (MLSys 2026)
- **Core idea:** "Lookahead retrieval" exploits the semantic overlap between a RAG pipeline's pre-retrieval query and its refined retrieval query to prefetch the likely IVF clusters from CPU to GPU during the pre-retrieval generation stage, enabling GPU-speed search with a fraction of the GPU memory a full on-GPU index would need.
- **Key reported result:** Up to 1.98x average end-to-end latency reduction (single-query) and 1.83x higher throughput (batched); 3.8x speedup scaling to 4 GPUs; runs a 61GB datastore + 16GB Llama-3-8B on a single 24GB RTX4090.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Designed specifically for IVF-style indices; HNSW is "less naturally compatible" (no explicit prefetchable units), though hybrid IVF-HNSW is argued to work.
  - Relies on CPU–GPU PCIe bandwidth being the bottleneck (traditional discrete GPU/CPU memory); less useful on unified-memory platforms (Apple M-series, NVIDIA Grace-Hopper) where GPU-only retrieval is already fast.
  - Effectiveness depends on semantic correlation between pre-retrieval and retrieval-stage queries (61–91% cluster overlap observed); pipelines without a pre-retrieval/query-rewrite stage get no lookahead signal.
  - Scheduler/cache overhead (~37ms prefetch scheduler, ~180ms cache-aware scheduler) and low hit-rate/benefit when budget <2GB or batch sizes grow large (per-query hit rate drops as batch size increases).
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No mechanism for predicting the retrieval query itself when there is no pre-retrieval stage (c.f. Predictive Prefetching paper) — TeleRAG purely exploits existing query correlation rather than learning to predict it, so one-shot/no-rewrite pipelines get zero benefit.
  - Caching/prefetching policy is static heuristic (fixed byte budget, greedy LRU/frequency-based eviction) rather than learned — an obvious combination point with learned predictors.
  - Does not address decode-time latency hiding (retrieval during generation, as in DRAGIN/FLARE-style iterative retrieval) — it only optimizes the single pre-retrieval→retrieval transition, not multi-round interleaved retrieval overlap with token generation.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Very feasible — explicitly validated on Llama-3.2-3B/Llama-3-8B with a single consumer RTX4090 and open-source code (FAISS + SGLang). This is one of the most directly reproducible baselines for a single-GPU Qwen8B/small-Llama setup; building a smaller-scale IVF datastore and testing lookahead retrieval requires no special hardware beyond what's already planned.

### HedraRAG (SOSP 2025)
- **Core idea:** A graph-based abstraction (RAGraph) represents heterogeneous multi-stage RAG workflows (one-shot, multi-hop, HyDE, iterative) and applies runtime graph transformations (node splitting, reordering, speculative edges) plus partial GPU index caching to pipeline fine-grained generation/retrieval sub-stages across concurrent requests on hybrid CPU–GPU systems.
- **Key reported result:** >1.5x and up to 5x throughput gains over LangChain/FlashRAG baselines; 2.2x–18.2x lower request latency at same request rate; GPU index caching gives 1.12x–1.49x speedup from cache hits.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Built on vLLM 0.6.6 + Faiss 1.9.0 — requires re-implementing/extending the vector search library's batched interface for fine-grained step-wise execution, a significant systems engineering lift.
  - Time-multiplexed (not simultaneous) execution of collocated stages is left as a simplification; "more complex strategies such as simultaneous execution" explicitly deferred to future work.
  - Speculative execution accuracy depends on semantic-similarity assumptions that can fail with topic drift (δs threshold tuning required); rollback on speculation error adds overhead.
  - Evaluated mainly on Llama-3-8B/Llama2-13B/OPT-30B on a CPU–GPU hybrid server (64-core EPYC + H100) — large-scale server-class hardware, not consumer/single-GPU.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No learned prediction of future retrieval needs — reordering/speculation is driven by embedding-distance heuristics (semantic similarity, hotspot frequency), not a trained predictor as in the Predictive Prefetching paper; combining the two is an open direction.
  - Partial GPU index caching uses simple frequency/hotspot heuristics; no comparison to TeleRAG's lookahead-prefetch-based cache warming — the two caching strategies (reactive-hotspot vs. predictive-lookahead) are never unified.
  - System complexity (custom graph scheduler, modified vLLM + Faiss internals) makes it hard to adopt incrementally; no ablation isolating which of the three techniques (partitioning vs. reordering/speculation vs. caching) matters most for single-query (non-batched) local latency, since the paper's gains are mostly throughput/concurrency-oriented, not single-query latency-oriented.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Partially feasible — the RAGraph abstraction and reordering/speculation ideas could be prototyped at small scale with Qwen8B on a single GPU, but the full system (fine-grained vLLM/Faiss co-scheduling, multi-request batching benefits) is designed for concurrent multi-request serving; its throughput gains won't manifest in a single-query, single-GPU local experiment, so only a subset of ideas (speculative generation/retrieval overlap) would be practically reproducible.

### RAGO (ISCA 2025)
- **Core idea:** RAGO introduces RAGSchema, a structured abstraction describing diverse RAG workload configurations (encoder, reranker, rewriter, retrieval frequency, database size), and a systematic scheduling optimizer that searches task placement (collocation vs. disaggregation), resource allocation, and batching policies to find Pareto-optimal RAG serving configurations.
- **Key reported result:** Up to 2x improvement in QPS/chip and 55% reduction in time-to-first-token versus RAG systems built as naive extensions of LLM-only serving systems; up to 52.5x–64.1x QPS/chip swing between good and bad resource allocation plans for the same workload.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Analysis and optimizer rely on an in-house analytical/simulated XPU cost model (systolic-array TPU-like accelerator, ScaNN-based retrieval model) rather than measured results on real deployed hardware/software stacks — a simulation-based study, not an implemented/open-source serving system.
  - Exhaustive search over placement/allocation/batching is combinatorial ("potentially millions of schedules"); scalability of the optimizer itself to larger pipelines or dynamic workloads is not addressed.
  - Focuses on steady-state throughput/TTFT trade-offs for data-center-scale, multi-accelerator, batched serving (16–128 XPUs per experiment) — not single-query, single-GPU latency.
  - Time-multiplexed collocation execution order modeling is simplified (no simultaneous execution modeled); iterative retrieval batching (Case III) shows decode-idleness tradeoffs that aren't fully solved by RAGO's scheduling alone.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No treatment of predictive/speculative retrieval overlap — RAGO assumes synchronous stage execution timed by batching/placement, not asynchronous prefetching; a predictive-prefetching layer (as in paper 1) could further shift the Pareto frontier RAGO computes.
  - The paper's central finding — that retrieval vs. inference bottleneck shifts depending on model size, query count, and database size — implies no single system design (TeleRAG's GPU-memory-saving approach, HedraRAG's pipelining, or predictive prefetching) is universally optimal; a gap exists in building an adaptive system that switches strategies based on RAGSchema-like workload characterization at runtime.
  - Being a scheduling/placement framework for data-center accelerator fleets, it offers no guidance for memory-constrained single-GPU local deployments where collocation/disaggregation choices are far more limited.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Low direct feasibility — RAGO's contribution is a scheduling optimizer and performance characterization across multiple accelerators (TPU-like XPUs, 16–128 chips), which doesn't map onto a single consumer GPU. However, its RAGSchema taxonomy and qualitative findings (e.g., retrieval dominates at small-model/multi-query hyperscale retrieval, encoder dominates at long-context) are directly useful as conceptual framing/motivation for a local Qwen8B experiment, even though the optimizer itself can't be run locally.

### VoiceAgentRAG (2026)
- **Core idea:** A dual-agent "Slow Thinker / Fast Talker" architecture decouples retrieval from response generation for multi-turn voice conversations: a background agent predicts 3–5 likely follow-up topics per turn and prefetches document chunks into an in-memory FAISS semantic cache (indexed by document embeddings, not query embeddings) during user think/listen time, while the foreground agent serves from this sub-millisecond cache and falls back to the vector DB only on misses.
- **Key reported result:** 75% overall cache hit rate (79% on warm turns), 316x retrieval speedup on cache hits (110ms→0.35ms), saving 16.5 seconds of cumulative retrieval latency across 150 cache hits in a 200-query, 10-scenario benchmark against Qdrant Cloud.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Explicitly states "LLM generation dominates total latency" — the 110ms retrieval savings is invisible against 500–8000ms LLM generation time with GPT-4o-mini via API; authors note faster inference (e.g., Groq, or local models) is needed to make retrieval savings the dominant factor.
  - Slow Thinker's topic predictions are not always correct, wasting prefetch/embedding-API calls; 5 predictions x 10 chunks/turn consumes significant embedding bandwidth.
  - Cache coherence is only eventual (TTL-based eviction, default 300s) — stale cached chunks possible if the underlying knowledge base changes mid-conversation.
  - Benefit is highly conversation-pattern-dependent: broad/random topic-shifting conversations (Mixed rapid-fire, Existing customer upgrade) get only 40–55% hit rate vs. 80–100% for single-topic deep-dives; needs 3–7s of inter-turn time for the background agent to complete its prefetch cycle — rapid back-and-forth (<2s) reduces benefit.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No learned/trained prediction model for follow-up topics — relies on prompting GPT-4o-mini to generate "document-style" topic descriptions; an open gap is replacing this with a cheaper, local, fine-tuned predictor (overlapping with the ICML Predictive Prefetching paper's approach) to reduce embedding-API cost and latency.
  - Cross-turn caching is unexplored in the context of token-level/intra-generation retrieval (DRAGIN/FLARE-style); VoiceAgentRAG only prefetches between turns, not within a single generation, so there's no synthesis between inter-turn and intra-generation prefetching strategies.
  - No evaluation with a local, low-latency LLM — the paper's own limitation note (generation dominates latency with API models) is exactly the gap a local Qwen8B/Llama setup could test: whether retrieval-latency savings become visible/meaningful once generation is fast and local.
- **Feasibility on local setup:** Highly feasible and directly relevant — the architecture (dual background/foreground agents + in-memory FAISS cache) is lightweight and framework-agnostic (supports OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Ollama for LLMs and FAISS/Qdrant for vector stores per their released code), so it can be run end-to-end locally with Qwen8B/small Llama on one GPU; this setup would also let the researcher directly test the paper's own flagged limitation (that local/fast generation would make retrieval savings dominant) as a novel contribution.

## Cross-cutting gaps within prefetching/pipelining (02_prefetch_pipelining)
- No paper combines a *learned* predictor of future retrieval need/query content (Predictive Prefetching, VoiceAgentRAG's Slow Thinker) with *systems-level* GPU-memory-aware prefetch scheduling (TeleRAG's lookahead retrieval, HedraRAG's partial GPU caching) — each paper picks one layer (algorithmic prediction vs. serving-system scheduling) but none unify both into a single stack.
- All five papers assume or evaluate retrieval against synchronous, document-level vector search (IVF/FAISS/Qdrant); none address prefetching for iterative, intra-generation retrieval at the token level combined with GPU-memory-constrained serving (e.g., DRAGIN/FLARE-style reactive triggering is explicitly only addressed by Predictive Prefetching and left as future work by HedraRAG/RAGO).
- Three papers (RAGO, HedraRAG, TeleRAG) are explicitly multi-GPU/data-center/batched-throughput-oriented; their reported speedups partly or fully depend on concurrency/batching that will not reproduce on a single-GPU, single-query local setup — a gap exists in understanding which prefetching/pipelining techniques retain benefit under single-query, single-GPU, small-model conditions (only TeleRAG and VoiceAgentRAG give single-query RTX4090-class numbers).
- No paper evaluates with fully local, small open-source models end-to-end in a latency-dominant-by-generation regime — VoiceAgentRAG explicitly flags that with API-based LLMs, generation latency masks retrieval savings; running the same prefetching ideas with a fast local Qwen8B/Llama decoder is an open, concretely testable gap.
- False-positive / wasted-prefetch cost is acknowledged but not solved anywhere: Predictive Prefetching reports 28% fully-wasted prefetches as future work, HedraRAG's speculative execution requires rollback on misprediction, VoiceAgentRAG wastes embedding-API calls on wrong topic predictions — a cross-cutting opportunity is a shared method for cheaply verifying/canceling speculative prefetches before committing resources.
- Caching policy design is inconsistent and mostly heuristic across all systems (LRU/frequency/hotspot in HedraRAG and TeleRAG, TTL+LRU in VoiceAgentRAG, no caching mechanism in Predictive Prefetching's result cache beyond simple dedup) — no paper explores a learned or unified cache-eviction/prefetch-budget policy that transfers across workload types (QA vs. multi-turn conversation vs. code).

## 3. Context Compression

### REFRAG (arXiv 2025, Meta Superintelligence Labs)
- **Core idea:** Replace most retrieved-context tokens with precomputed, lightweight-encoder chunk embeddings fed directly into the decoder, using an RL policy to selectively "expand" only the few chunks that matter back into full tokens.
- **Key reported result:** 30.85x TTFT acceleration (3.75x over prior SOTA CEPE) with no loss in perplexity at compression rate k=32; 16.53x TTFT speedup at k=16 with a 9.3% average log-perplexity improvement over CEPE; up to 6.78x throughput gain vs. LLaMA-2-7B; also extends effective context 16x.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):** Requires a non-trivial continual pre-training recipe (reconstruction task + curriculum learning) that the authors show is "essential" — without curriculum learning the reconstruction task fails to converge at all; compression rate 64 is "overly aggressive" causing clear performance regression, implying a practical ceiling around k=32; the RL-trained selective-compression policy and encoder-decoder alignment require large-scale training (8 nodes x 8 H100s, 20B-token CPT corpus); relies on block-diagonal/low cross-passage attention being present, i.e., benefits are tied to RAG's sparse-attention structure and may not generalize to tasks with dense cross-chunk dependencies.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - The encoder-decoder alignment (CPT + reconstruction + curriculum) is itself expensive and model/architecture-specific (RoBERTa encoder + LLaMA decoder); there's no cheap recipe for aligning arbitrary small open-source LLMs (e.g., Qwen3-8B) without repeating this multi-stage pretraining.
  - The RL policy for selective expansion is trained on perplexity reward only, not downstream task accuracy, leaving a gap versus performance-driven RL approaches like CORE-RAG.
  - No analysis of combining REFRAG-style embedding compression with evidence-filtering/lexical methods (ECoRAG, CORE-RAG) that remove entire irrelevant documents before chunk-embedding compression.
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Not easily reproducible as-is — REFRAG needs multi-stage CPT with a 20B-token corpus on 64 H100s plus RL training of a selective-compression policy; a single-GPU researcher could only approximate it (e.g., skip CPT and directly fine-tune a small off-the-shelf encoder for chunk embeddings on a narrow downstream task), losing most of the reported gains and guarantees.

### CORE-RAG (ICML 2026 submission)
- **Core idea:** Train a lightweight generative compressor with RL (GRPO), using the downstream black-box LLM's actual task performance (EM/F1) as the reward — rather than proxy heuristics like ROUGE or mutual information — to produce query-focused document summaries.
- **Key reported result:** At ~3-6% of original token count (e.g., 46-52 tokens vs. 712-1425 for top-5/10 docs), CORE improves average EM by 1.5-6.9 points over full-context RAG across NQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA/2WikiMultihopQA with Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct as the generator; outperforms a 671B-parameter DeepSeek-V3 compressor using only a 1.5B compressor.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):** Reward design does not generalize automatically to open-ended generation tasks — authors note summarization/dialogue/long-form settings need entirely different reward functions (BARTScore, LLM-as-judge, coherence metrics), which is future work, not solved; RL training requires online rollouts through the frozen downstream LLM for every training example, which is compute- and latency-heavy relative to supervised fine-tuning; EM-based reward is sparse (motivating an added F1 term, itself hyperparameter-sensitive, α tuned per dataset in [0.2,0.5]); evaluated only on extractive QA, not shown effective for multi-hop reasoning chains requiring many combined facts beyond the tested HotpotQA/2Wiki benchmarks at scale.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - GRPO-based RL compressor training needs repeated forward passes through a 14B "judge" LLM per rollout group — this is the single biggest barrier to adapting the method cheaply; no distilled/offline reward model is proposed to remove this dependency at training time.
  - No combination explored with latency-focused methods (REFRAG) — CORE reduces token count but still re-encodes the compressor's text output as normal tokens into the frozen LLM, meaning it does not reduce the cross-attention/KV-cache cost structurally the way embedding-based approaches do.
  - The reward-driven paradigm is black-box-LLM-specific per training; transfer experiments (Table 3/4) show good but not perfect cross-LLM/cross-dataset transfer, suggesting compressors still partially overfit to the specific judge LLM's quirks.
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Reasonably feasible — the compressor is only 1.5B-3B (Qwen2.5-1.5B/Llama3.2-1B-3B tested) and training uses GRPO over a fixed downstream LLM; this is lighter than REFRAG's continual pretraining, but RL rollouts through a 14B frozen judge model are still GPU/VRAM-heavy for a single consumer GPU — would likely need to swap the Qwen2.5-14B judge for a smaller judge (e.g., Qwen3-8B) and reduce rollout count (G) to fit.

### ACC-RAG (EMNLP 2025 Findings)
- **Core idea:** A hierarchical compressor produces multi-granularity document embeddings (nested at different compression rates), and an RL/supervised-trained "selector" dynamically decides, at inference, how many embeddings to feed the decoder before stopping — adapting compression rate per-query instead of using one fixed ratio.
- **Key reported result:** >4x faster inference (FTIT) than standard RAG while matching or exceeding RAG accuracy on 4 of 5 datasets (NQ, TriviaQA, SQuAD improve significantly; WQ/TREC within noise); compression rates as aggressive as x128 tested, with adaptive scheme achieving CR ~630-878 tokens average vs. 1081+ for fixed-rate plug-in baselines.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself, explicit "Limitations" section):** The selector is "currently the largest bottleneck in the entire framework" — only preliminary exploration of its design; compressor and selector are trained separately (not jointly), potentially misaligning the two modules and limiting overall performance; the paper only studies adaptive context selection, lacking controlled experimental analysis of why/when it helps (left to future work); due to compute constraints, never tested on larger models or longer texts, limiting applicability claims.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - Joint training of compressor + selector (vs. the current two-stage pipeline) is explicitly flagged as unexplored — an opportunity for an end-to-end differentiable or RL-joint objective.
  - Selector accuracy (~70% sequence accuracy per Table 10) is the main bottleneck; no work combines ACC-RAG's adaptive stopping with CORE-RAG's performance-driven (task-reward) training signal, which could directly optimize the selector against downstream EM instead of a proxy classification loss.
  - Untested at longer-context / larger-LLM scale — leaves open whether adaptive granularity selection holds up when documents are much longer (REFRAG/ECoRAG's 100+ document, longer-context regime).
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Feasible with moderate effort — backbone is Mistral-7B-Instruct with LoRA fine-tuning (1xA100 80G reported) for the compressor, and the selector is a small 4-layer/4-head transformer trained in ~1 hour; this is squarely within single-GPU, small-model budget and a good candidate to reproduce/extend with Qwen8B/Llama as backbone.

### ECoRAG (ACL 2025 Findings)
- **Core idea:** Compress retrieved documents by scoring each sentence's "evidentiality" (whether it causally supports generating the correct answer, distinguishing strong evidence / weak evidence / distractor) via a lightweight trained compressor and evaluator, then adaptively add more evidence sentences only if a small evaluator judges the current compression insufficient.
- **Key reported result:** On NQ/TQA/WQ with GPT-4o-mini as reader and 100 retrieved documents, ECoRAG outperforms strongest compression baseline (CompAct) by +0.77/+1.38/+0.40 EM points while using fewer tokens (e.g., 632 tokens vs. 13,905 for full 100-doc context — ~95% token reduction); reduces total latency by 7.32h vs. standard RAG on NQ test set (Table 4), using only a 770M evaluator and lightweight extractive compressor.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself, explicit "Limitation" section):** Mining evidentiality labels is computationally expensive — requires multiple LLM inferences per training question, which is costly at training-data-construction time (though a one-time cost); framework currently scoped to ODQA and explicitly says extending to other tasks like summarization "may be necessary" and would require redefining evidentiality around different metrics (left as future work, not solved); iterative evidentiality-reflection loop can increase latency in worst-case scenarios (needs a token-limit safeguard to avoid infinite loops when gold evidence isn't retrieved at all).
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No generalization shown beyond extractive QA (summarization, multi-turn dialogue, agentic RAG) — the evidentiality definition is tied to "does removing this sentence break correct-answer generation," which doesn't trivially transfer to generative/open-ended tasks.
  - The evidentiality-mining step depends on repeatedly querying a large reader LLM (Flan-UL2 20B) to build training labels — a real barrier for researchers wanting to adapt this to a new domain/dataset cheaply; no cheaper/self-supervised labeling scheme is proposed.
  - Like ACC-RAG, ECoRAG's adaptive iterative compression (add-more-evidence loop) is sequential at inference and not combined with embedding-based speedups (REFRAG) — the resulting compressed text is still plain text re-fed through normal attention, not a KV-cache/latency optimization at the architecture level.
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Highly feasible — the trainable compressor is built on Contriever (~110M) and the evidentiality evaluator is Flan-T5-large (770M); VRAM usage reported as compressor 110M + evaluator 770M + reader ≥8B (Table 9), which fits a single consumer/workstation GPU alongside a small reader like Qwen3-8B. The main cost is the one-time evidentiality-label mining pass over training QA pairs using an LLM judge, which could be done with a smaller local judge instead of Flan-UL2/GPT-4o-mini to stay fully local.

## Cross-cutting gaps within context compression (03_context_compression)
- **No method combines architecture-level latency reduction (REFRAG's embedding-based KV-cache/attention savings) with task-performance-driven selection (CORE-RAG's RL-on-accuracy) or evidentiality filtering (ECoRAG/ACC-RAG)** — these are largely orthogonal axes (what to compress vs. how it's fed to the decoder) that remain unexplored together, despite REFRAG's own discussion section noting prompt-compression approaches are "complementary" and "can be integrated."
- **Training/initialization cost for compressors varies wildly and none report a cheap, from-scratch recipe for small open LLMs (Qwen8B, small Llama) on a single GPU** — REFRAG needs multi-node CPT; CORE-RAG needs online RL rollouts through a frozen judge LLM; ACC-RAG and ECoRAG are the most tractable (LoRA/Contriever + 7B class backbones) but still need custom label-generation or reward pipelines.
- **Compressor-selector/evaluator misalignment from two-stage (non-joint) training appears repeatedly** — ACC-RAG explicitly flags this as its main limitation, and CORE-RAG's distillation-then-RL warm-start has a similar two-phase disconnect; no paper in this set jointly optimizes retrieval-compression-generation end-to-end.
- **Generalization beyond single-hop/extractive QA is weak across the board** — REFRAG's RAG fine-tuning and ECoRAG's evidentiality definition are both validated mainly on NQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA-style QA; none convincingly demonstrate robustness on long-form generation, summarization-as-RAG, or multi-turn agentic settings at the same compression ratios (REFRAG does test summarization/multi-turn but with smaller headline gains).
- **Adaptive/selective compression (ACC-RAG, ECoRAG, REFRAG's RL policy) all add inference-time control overhead (selector, evaluator, iterative reflection) that is only partially characterized** — latency/VRAM overhead of the controller itself is measured in each paper but never benchmarked head-to-head against each other, so it's unclear which adaptive mechanism is most efficient under a single-GPU budget.
- **Reward/training signal design for "what counts as good compression" is unsolved outside EM/F1-style QA** — CORE-RAG explicitly defers open-ended reward design to future work, and ECoRAG's evidentiality labels are QA-answer-specific; a transferable, cheap reward signal for compression quality (that doesn't require an LLM judge or gold answers) is a clear open gap directly relevant to a local experimental setup without large-scale labeling budgets.

## 4. Visual-Document Retrieval Speed

### HPC-ColPali (arXiv preprint, Jul 2025)
- **Core idea:** Compress ColPali's multi-vector patch embeddings via K-Means quantization (1-byte centroid indices), prune patches at query time using VLM attention weights, and optionally binarize centroid indices for Hamming-distance search.
- **Key reported result:** Up to 32x storage compression (57x with binary encoding) with <2% nDCG@10 loss; 30–50% lower query latency under HNSW (e.g., 120ms→60ms on ViDoRe); in a RAG pipeline, 30% hallucination reduction and 2x end-to-end latency reduction.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Explicitly states: "All numerical results presented herein are estimated based on the theoretical advantages of HPC-ColPali's design... aiming to demonstrate its potential for a Q1 journal publication" — i.e., results are not from real experiments, a major caveat for any researcher citing this paper.
  - No adaptive pruning — the pruning ratio p is a fixed, manually tuned hyperparameter, not responsive to query complexity or document type.
  - No mechanism for updating K-Means codebooks as document collections evolve (static codebook assumption).
  - Authors themselves list future work: hierarchical PQ, adaptive pruning policies, hardware acceleration — none implemented.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - Because results are simulated/estimated rather than measured, there is no validated real-world latency/accuracy tradeoff curve for K-Means quantization + attention pruning on ColPali-style models — an actual empirical replication is itself a contribution.
  - No comparison of quantization granularity (K) vs. pruning rate (p) jointly optimized under an actual latency budget.
  - Unclear how attention-weight-based pruning interacts with Qwen-family VLMs' known attention-sink/bias patterns (not analyzed).
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Highly feasible and attractive as a base for real experiments — the method only needs offline K-Means (cheap, CPU-feasible) and attention-weight extraction from a HF ColQwen/ColPali checkpoint, no training required. Since the paper's own numbers are admittedly estimated, actually running this on a single GPU with real ViDoRe-style data would produce a legitimate, citable empirical result and is a strong, low-barrier starting point.

### DocPC (arXiv:2608.25434, Aug 2026)
- **Core idea:** Shift visual document retrieval from page-level to document-level by selecting K representative pages (e.g., first-4), compositing them into a single grid image, and encoding/indexing the whole document with one VLM forward pass instead of one pass per page.
- **Key reported result:** 10.1x reduction in indexed images/vectors/storage and 7.7x reduction in end-to-end indexing time; DocPC-ColQwen achieves NDCG@5 of 44.09 on the new DocViRe benchmark vs. 38.91 for the strongest page-level baseline.
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself):**
  - Fixed page budget: ablations show more representative pages (K=16) do not help and can hurt (NDCG drops to 36.59 vs. 40.66 for K=4) — the representative-page selection does not scale with document length or information density.
  - Representative page selection strategies (First-K, Boundary, Base-CLIP) are heuristic/static; no learned or query-adaptive page selection.
  - Grid composition at test time is fixed (2x2); no analysis of how this interacts with documents that have highly distributed, non-localized relevant content beyond the chosen pages.
  - Benchmark (DocViRe) is LLM-generated/validated rather than naturally occurring queries, which may not fully represent real document-retrieval query distributions.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No mechanism to dynamically decide K (how many representative pages) per document based on length/complexity — a fixed K=4 is a blunt instrument and a combination with HPC-ColPali's attention-based pruning could allow adaptive within-grid patch pruning.
  - Document-level compression (DocPC) and patch-level compression (HPC-ColPali/PULSAR pooling) address orthogonal axes (page count vs. patch count per page) — no existing paper combines grid composition with patch quantization/pruning for compounded latency gains.
  - No reported absolute query latency numbers (only storage/indexing time) — unclear how much query-time late-interaction compute is actually saved versus just storage.
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Very feasible — built directly on ColQwen with supervised fine-tuning on a modest benchmark (DocViRe, ~15K queries); fine-tuning a small ColQwen variant with multi-positive InfoNCE + listwise loss is realistic on a single GPU, and the DocViRe benchmark/code are stated to be publicly released, lowering the reproduction barrier substantially.

### PULSAR (arXiv:2608.28572, Aug 2026)
- **Core idea:** A production vision-first retrieval system that pools ColPali-style multi-vector page embeddings into a two-stage index (mean row/column pooling for prefetch via HNSW, hierarchical pooling for exact MaxSim rerank), plus decoupled low/high-DPI rendering for embedding vs. answer-time grounding.
- **Key reported result:** 15.1x reduction in median vector-search latency (752.7ms→49.9ms) at <0.01 NDCG@10/Recall@10 loss; ~88x higher QPS under concurrent load vs. unpooled index; ~20x cheaper ingestion per page than OCR+verbalisation baseline; deployed at firm scale (78K documents, 2.4M pages).
- **Stated limitations (from the paper itself, explicit "Limitations" section):**
  - "Late interaction stays heavier than single-vector retrieval" — pooling and quantization reduce but do not eliminate the fundamental overhead of storing/scoring many vectors per page vs. a single dot product.
  - Findings are corpus-, baseline-, and backbone-specific: measured on one firm's chart/table-heavy private deal documents with ColQwen3-4B; DPI sweet spot and cost/latency numbers may not transfer to other corpora, chart densities, or backbones; equivalence tests were run on public ViDoRe V3, not the firm's own documents (unconfirmed transfer).
  - Ingestion cost estimate (~20x) is ingestion-only and excludes serving/answer-time inference costs.
  - Only compares against one baseline (OCR+verbalisation); does not compare against alternative vision-first stacks (OCR-free document transformers) or generate-and-encode hybrids.
  - Answer-time DPI ablation uses same-family LLM for question generation and answering, risking shared visual blind spots; effective sample size for cross-deck generalization is only 2 decks.
- **Research gap (your analysis):**
  - No combination of PULSAR's pooling/quantization with document-level aggregation (as in DocPC) — pooling reduces per-page vector count but still indexes every page; combining with representative-page selection could compound savings for very long documents.
  - The paper explicitly avoids fine-tuning ("PULSAR uses a pretrained ColQwen3-4B with no firm-specific fine-tuning") — leaves open whether lightweight fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA on a small Llama/Qwen) could recover additional quality at equal or lower latency, an unexplored tradeoff space.
  - No exploration of attention-guided dynamic pruning (as in HPC-ColPali) combined with their pooling — pooling is static/uniform (row/column mean) rather than content-adaptive.
- **Feasibility on local setup (Qwen8B/small Llama, single GPU, no large-scale pretraining):** Partially feasible — the pooling/quantization/two-stage HNSW design is reproducible at small scale with HF ColQwen checkpoints and Qdrant/FAISS locally, and requires no training (frozen backbone). However, the production-scale claims (concurrent load, 2.4M pages, firm deal corpus) cannot be replicated on a single GPU/small corpus; a local reproduction would need to focus on the algorithmic pooling+quantization ablation (§5.1–5.2) on public ViDoRe data rather than the deployment-scale throughput claims.

## Cross-cutting gaps within visual-document retrieval (04_visual_document_retrieval)
- All three papers attack a different axis of the same cost problem (patch-level compression in HPC-ColPali, page-count/document-level composition in DocPC, cross-stage pooling+quantization in PULSAR) but none combine axes — a stacked approach (document-level page selection + patch pruning/quantization + pooled two-stage indexing) is unexplored and is a concrete, implementable research gap for a local single-GPU study.
- Reproducibility/rigor varies sharply: HPC-ColPali's numbers are explicitly self-described as theoretical estimates rather than measured results, while DocPC and PULSAR report real experiments — any lit review or gap analysis should flag HPC-ColPali's results as unverified and treat it as a design proposal rather than an empirical baseline.
- None of the three papers study adaptive/query-conditioned resource allocation (adaptive pruning ratio, adaptive K pages, adaptive pooling factor) — all use fixed, manually chosen hyperparameters (p, K, pool factor), despite all three explicitly naming this as future work (HPC-ColPali) or an acknowledged limitation (DocPC's page-budget ablation showing non-monotonic behavior).
- Latency numbers across papers are not directly comparable (different hardware: A100 cluster vs. half-A10 production vs. unspecified for DocPC) and none report latency/accuracy curves on a common small-model/single-GPU setup — there is a clear opportunity for a standardized, reproducible single-GPU benchmark comparing these three compression strategies head-to-head using an open small model (e.g., ColQwen2/ColQwen2.5 with Qwen 8B backbone).
- All three rely on late-interaction/MaxSim scoring inherited from ColBERT/ColPali; none explore replacing or hybridizing late interaction with single-vector first-stage retrieval beyond simple pooling (PULSAR mentions HEAVEN as related work but does not adopt it) — a single-vector-first, late-interaction-rerank hybrid tuned for small local models remains underexplored for latency reduction specifically in the RAG-serving context (as opposed to pure IR benchmarks).

## 5. Background Surveys

### RAG Survey (Sharma, preprint under review at ACM TOIS, arXiv:2506.00054, 2025)
- **Scope:** A comprehensive taxonomy-driven survey of RAG architectures (retriever-centric, generator-centric, hybrid, robustness-oriented), synthesizing enhancements, comparative benchmark performance, and evaluation practices.
- **What it says about latency/efficiency specifically:**
  - Frames inefficiency as coming from three sources: memory overhead from storing/indexing retrieved content, latency from the retrieval-processing pipeline itself, and redundancy in passage selection (Sec 4.3, "Efficiency Enhancements").
  - Groups mitigations into four buckets: (1) sparse/retrieval-aware context selection (Sparse RAG, R²AG — drop low-signal tokens before self-attention to reduce input length), (2) inference acceleration (FiD-Light compresses passages for faster decoding; Speculative Pipelining overlaps retrieval and generation, cutting time-to-first-token 20–30% but risking "speculative hallucinations" without fallback mechanisms), (3) caching/redundancy reduction (RAGCache: hierarchical KV-tensor caching; PGDSF: prefix-aware eviction — both lose effectiveness on long-tail/non-repetitive query distributions), and (4) retrieval faithfulness methods (RAE) that trade efficiency for accuracy via retraining.
  - Explicitly notes a recurring trade-off: filtering/reranking/granularity techniques that improve accuracy (e.g., retriever-centric adaptation, multi-round retrieval) also "introduce trade-offs in latency, redundancy, and sensitivity to ambiguous queries" (Sec 3.1) — efficiency gains and accuracy gains are frequently in tension across the surveyed literature, and most efficiency techniques (RAGCache, Speculative Pipelining) have only been demonstrated on large backbones (LLaMA2-7B/13B/70B, GPT-3.5/4, Mixtral-8x7B) — no coverage of small-model/single-GPU serving regimes.
  - Ablation studies (Sec 5.4) show compression/caching ablations increase inference time up to 4x when removed, underscoring that these are not optional add-ons but core to production viability — yet the survey treats efficiency as one axis among five (retrieval, filtering, efficiency, robustness, reranking) rather than a central research question in its own right.
- **Open challenges/future directions flagged by the survey (related to latency):**
  - Sec 7.1 "Retrieval Adaptivity and Semantic Alignment": "Current RAG architectures often rely on static retrieval policies and fixed embedding transformations, limiting their adaptability... Future systems must support dynamically calibrated retrieval strategies that adjust depth, modality, and source selection in response to task difficulty."
  - Sec 4.3 flags "future speculative decoding architectures... that balance responsiveness and reliability in low-latency applications" as an open line of work (referencing Speculative Pipelining's unresolved hallucination risk).
  - Sec 6 (Evaluation) notes the field lacks standardized, efficiency-aware benchmarking: "RetrievalQA... tests whether models can intelligently toggle retrieval based on query uncertainty, supporting the development of resource-efficient, retrieval-aware systems that avoid introducing unnecessary context" — flagged as an emerging but still underdeveloped benchmark category.
  - Sec 7 closing future-directions list (adaptivity, robustness, multi-hop compositionality, cross-domain generalization, explainability/trust) does not include a dedicated "efficiency/latency" future-direction subsection — efficiency is treated as solved-enough to fold into adaptivity, which itself is a gap the researcher can point to.
- **How this should shape the researcher's gap framing:**
  - The survey's own taxonomy (Table 1) shows essentially every reported efficiency method (Sparse RAG, FiD-Light, Speculative Pipelining, RAGCache, PGDSF, RAE) was evaluated on 7B–70B+ models or GPT-3.5/4 — the researcher can position small open-source models (Qwen 8B, small Llama) on single-GPU as an underexplored regime where these latency techniques' cost/benefit trade-offs (e.g., caching overhead vs. hit rate, compression accuracy loss) have not been empirically validated.
  - Because the survey explicitly flags that efficiency techniques and accuracy/robustness techniques trade off against each other (Sec 3.1, Sec 4.3), the gap framing can position the researcher's work as measuring that trade-off directly for small-model local deployments, where compute headroom to "pay" for accuracy-preserving overhead (reranking, multi-round retrieval) is much lower than in the surveyed GPU-cluster settings.
  - The survey calls for "dynamically calibrated retrieval strategies" (adaptive retrieval depth/triggering) as future work (Sec 7.1) but only demonstrates these (DRAGIN, FLARE, TA-ARE) on large backbones — the researcher can frame their contribution as testing whether adaptive/dynamic retrieval triggering is even more valuable (or differently valuable) as a latency lever specifically under single-GPU small-model constraints, where every unnecessary retrieval call is proportionally more expensive.

### Agentic RAG Survey (Singh, Ehtesham, Kumar, Talaei Khoei, Vasilakos, arXiv:2501.09136v4, 2025/2026)
- **Scope:** An analytical survey proposing a principled taxonomy of Agentic RAG systems (single-agent, multi-agent, hierarchical/corrective, graph-based) organized by agent cardinality, control structure, autonomy, and knowledge representation, with applied case studies and open-challenges synthesis.
- **What it says about latency/efficiency specifically:**
  - Table 3 (Sec 9) explicitly ranks architectures on a "Latency" dimension: Single-Agent RAG = Low latency / low engineering complexity; Multi-Agent = Moderate; Hierarchical/Corrective = High; Graph-Based = Moderate-to-high — i.e., latency cost scales directly with reasoning/coordination sophistication.
  - Sec 10.1 ("Agentic RAG Is Not Always the Right Default") states plainly: agentic RAG "introduces coordination complexity, latency, and computational cost... For simple fact retrieval or well-scoped queries, modular RAG pipelines provide sufficient performance with lower overhead" — latency is framed as the direct price of adding autonomy/agentic control, not an incidental engineering detail.
  - Sec 10.2 reiterates the trade-off qualitatively: "hierarchical architectures introduce oversight at the expense of complexity... corrective workflows improve accuracy but add latency, while adaptive architectures trade correctness guarantees for responsiveness" — framing latency reduction and answer correctness as a dial, not a solved problem.
  - Sec 12.4 ("Computational Cost, Efficiency, and Sustainability") directly names the cause: "Multi-agent collaboration and iterative retrieval increase latency and resource consumption."
- **Open challenges/future directions flagged by the survey (related to latency):**
  - Sec 12.4 (near-verbatim): "Future research must explore cost-aware planning, adaptive inference, and lightweight coordination—dynamically adjusting active agents, selectively invoking tools, and optimizing retrieval depth based on task complexity."
  - Sec 12.1 flags that "current orchestration relies on heuristic rules and prompt engineering with limited convergence guarantees" for multi-agent coordination — an implicit latency/efficiency gap since uncontrolled orchestration loops are a known latency driver.
  - Sec 12.2 calls for "process-aware evaluation" including "cost efficiency" as a benchmarking dimension that is currently missing — "output-level metrics... are insufficient... Standardized benchmarks evaluating reasoning trajectories, planning depth, adaptability, robustness under noisy retrieval, and cost efficiency are needed."
  - The survey's conclusion (Sec 13) lists "computational efficiency" as one of five substantial open challenges remaining in the field, alongside coordination, evaluation, memory management, and governance — confirming efficiency/latency is explicitly unresolved rather than a mature subtopic.
- **How this should shape the researcher's gap framing:**
  - This survey gives a ready-made, citable framing device: Table 3's latency-vs-architecture-complexity trade-off. The researcher can position their experiments (likely testing simpler single-agent or lightweight pipeline variants on Qwen 8B/small Llama) as empirically quantifying that trade-off curve at the small-model end, which the survey only characterizes qualitatively (Low/Moderate/High) without benchmark numbers tied to resource-constrained hardware.
  - Sec 12.4's explicit call for "adaptive inference" and "selectively invoking tools... based on task complexity" is a direct, almost pre-written research gap statement the researcher can cite and then narrow to a single-GPU/small-LLM setting — none of the survey's examples (LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, Vertex AI, watsonx.ai) are evaluated under hardware-constrained or small-model conditions, so this is a legitimate and clearly-flagged gap, not just a reasonable-sounding one.
  - Since the survey stresses that agentic complexity should be "guided by task complexity" (Sec 10.1) and that unconstrained autonomy "requires explicit constraints" (Sec 10.4), the researcher's gap framing can argue that for small, locally-hosted LLMs — which have less headroom to absorb agentic/iterative retrieval overhead than the GPT-4/Gemini-class systems implicitly assumed in most cited frameworks — latency-aware constraint design (bounded retrieval depth, selective tool/agent invocation) is not just a nice-to-have but a necessity, making single-GPU small-model RAG latency a distinct and underserved research niche rather than a scaled-down version of the large-model problem.
